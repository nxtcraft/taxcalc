What Changed for FY 2026-27?

  • Slabs unchanged: Budget 2026 kept the FY 2025-26 new-regime slabs, the ₹75,000 standard deduction and the ₹60,000 Section 87A rebate.
  • New law: the Income Tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026. "Previous year" and "assessment year" are replaced by a single tax year — FY 2026-27 is Tax Year 2026-27.
  • Zero tax up to ₹12.75 lakh salary in the new regime continues.

Key Deductions Under the Old Regime

  • Section 80C (₹1,50,000): PPF, ELSS, EPF employee share, 5-year FD, NSC, home loan principal, life insurance, children's tuition fees, Sukanya Samriddhi.
  • HRA exemption: least of (a) HRA received, (b) rent paid minus 10% of basic, (c) 50% of basic in metros / 40% elsewhere. HRA calculator →
  • Section 80CCD(1B) — NPS: extra ₹50,000 over the 80C limit. NPS calculator →
  • Home loan interest: up to ₹2 lakh a year for a self-occupied house.
  • Section 80D — health insurance: ₹25,000 for self/family (₹50,000 if senior), plus up to ₹50,000 for senior-citizen parents.
  • 80TTA / 80TTB: ₹10,000 on savings interest; ₹50,000 on all deposit interest for senior citizens.

What Is Allowed in the New Regime?

  • Standard deduction ₹75,000 for salaried employees and pensioners (₹25,000 on family pension).
  • Employer NPS (80CCD(2)) up to 14% of basic + DA — the biggest legal saving in the new regime.
  • Employer EPF contribution (within limits), gratuity and leave encashment exemptions on exit. Gratuity calculator →
  • Official travel and conveyance reimbursements.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which tax regime is better in India for FY 2026-27?

For most salaried people the new regime. Salary up to ₹12.75 lakh pays zero tax under it (₹12L rebate limit + ₹75,000 standard deduction). The old regime only wins if your deductions are large: roughly ₹4.5L+ at ₹10L salary, ₹5.5L+ at ₹15L, ₹7L+ at ₹20L and about ₹8L+ at ₹25L and above (80C, HRA, home loan interest, 80D, NPS combined).

Did Budget 2026 change income tax slabs?

No. Budget 2026 kept the FY 2025-26 slabs, the ₹75,000 standard deduction and the ₹60,000 Section 87A rebate unchanged for FY 2026-27. The main change is that the Income Tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026, and 'assessment year' is now called 'tax year'.

What is the 87A rebate for FY 2026-27?

Under the new regime, tax is fully rebated (up to ₹60,000) if taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less, so salaried income up to ₹12.75 lakh is tax-free. Just above ₹12 lakh, marginal relief caps your tax at the amount by which income exceeds ₹12 lakh. Under the old regime, the rebate is ₹12,500 for taxable income up to ₹5 lakh.

Can I switch between old and new regime every year?

Yes, salaried employees without business income can choose each year when filing. The new regime is the default. Taxpayers with business or professional income can switch back to the old regime only once.

What is the surcharge on income tax?

Surcharge applies to high earners: 10% (₹50L–₹1Cr), 15% (₹1Cr–₹2Cr), 25% (₹2Cr–₹5Cr) and 37% above ₹5Cr under the old regime. Under the new regime surcharge is capped at 25%. A 4% health and education cess then applies on tax plus surcharge.
