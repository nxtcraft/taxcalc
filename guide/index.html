Old vs New Regime at a Glance

The fundamental choice every Indian taxpayer faces is between two parallel tax systems. The New Regime offers lower slab rates but removes almost all deductions. The Old Regime taxes income at higher rates but lets you legally reduce your taxable income through investments, rent, home loans and more.

87A Rebate: If your taxable income after standard deduction is ₹7 lakh or below, the entire tax is waived. Effectively, anyone earning up to ₹7.75 lakh (₹7L + ₹75K standard deduction) pays zero tax in the new regime.

Key Deductions Under the Old Regime

The old regime's advantage comes entirely from deductions. Here are the major ones:

  • Section 80C (₹1,50,000 limit): PPF contributions, ELSS mutual funds, 5-year FD, NSC, home loan principal repayment, life insurance premium (LIC), children's tuition fees, Sukanya Samriddhi. This one section alone can save ₹46,800 in tax (30% slab + cess).
  • Standard Deduction (₹50,000): Flat deduction for all salaried employees and pensioners — no proof required. Available in old regime; new regime gives ₹75,000.
  • HRA Exemption: If you live in a rented house, the minimum of three values: (a) actual HRA received, (b) rent paid minus 10% of basic salary, (c) 50% of basic (metro: Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata) or 40% of basic (non-metro).
  • Section 80CCD(1B) — NPS: An additional ₹50,000 deduction over and above the 80C limit for voluntary NPS contributions. At 30% slab, this saves ₹15,600 in tax.
  • Home Loan Interest (Section 24b): Up to ₹2 lakh deduction per year on interest paid for a self-occupied property. Under construction property has different rules.
  • Section 80D — Health Insurance: ₹25,000 for self/family below 60 years, ₹50,000 if any insured person is a senior citizen. ₹5,000 additional for preventive health checkup.
  • Section 80TTA/80TTB — Savings Interest: ₹10,000 deduction on savings account interest (80TTA) for below 60; ₹50,000 on all interest income for senior citizens (80TTB).

What Deductions Are Allowed in the New Regime?

Despite stripping most deductions, the new regime still allows a few:

  • Standard Deduction: ₹75,000 — available from FY 2024-25 (increased from ₹50,000).
  • Employer NPS contribution (Section 80CCD(2)): Up to 14% of basic salary if your employer contributes to NPS on your behalf. This is a significant benefit for government employees and those with employer-NPS schemes.
  • Gratuity exemption and leave encashment: Both are exempt up to prescribed limits.
  • Family pension standard deduction: ₹15,000 or one-third of pension, whichever is lower.
  • Conveyance and travel allowances for official duty remain exempt.

Surcharge and Cess — The Hidden Tax

Many people overlook surcharge and cess when estimating their tax liability. These add meaningfully to high incomes.

Income RangeSurcharge RateMax Surcharge (New Regime)
Up to ₹50 lakhNilNil
₹50L – ₹1 crore10%10%
₹1Cr – ₹2 crore15%15%
₹2Cr – ₹5 crore25%25% (capped)
Above ₹5 crore37% (old) / 25% (new)25% (capped)

Health and Education Cess: 4% is applied on (basic tax + surcharge) for every taxpayer, regardless of income level. This means a 30% slab taxpayer effectively pays 30% × 1.04 = 31.2%. At ₹50L+ with 10% surcharge, the effective rate is 30% × 1.10 × 1.04 = 34.32%.

The new regime caps surcharge at 25% (vs 37% under old regime for incomes above ₹5Cr), making the new regime more attractive for very high earners.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which tax regime is better in India FY 2024-25?

The new regime is better if your total deductions are below ₹3.75 lakh. For people earning up to ₹7.75 lakh with no special deductions, the new regime means zero tax. For those with a home loan, HRA, and maxed-out 80C, the old regime can save ₹50,000–₹1,00,000 or more annually at higher incomes.

Can I switch between old and new regime every year?

Yes, salaried employees with no business income can switch regimes every year. Just inform your employer at the beginning of the financial year. Business owners and freelancers can switch to the old regime only once after opting for the new regime.

What is the 87A tax rebate?

Section 87A gives a full rebate so your net tax is zero if taxable income is ≤ ₹5L (old regime) or ≤ ₹7L (new regime). With the ₹75K standard deduction in the new regime, anyone earning ≤ ₹7.75L gross pays zero income tax.

Is the standard deduction of ₹75,000 available in the new regime?

Yes, from FY 2024-25. Budget 2024 extended the standard deduction to the new regime and raised it from ₹50,000 to ₹75,000. It applies automatically to all salaried employees and pensioners — no claim needed.

What is surcharge on income tax?

Surcharge is an additional levy on income tax for high earners: 10% (income ₹50L–₹1Cr), 15% (₹1Cr–₹2Cr), 25% (₹2Cr+). Under the new regime, surcharge is capped at 25%. Then 4% health and education cess applies on (tax + surcharge) for all taxpayers.
