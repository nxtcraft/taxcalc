Old vs New Tax Regime FY 2025-26 — What Changed in Budget 2025?

Budget 2025 significantly revised the New Tax Regime making it the default and more attractive option for most salaried individuals. Key changes: basic exemption raised to ₹4 lakh, new slabs introduced (5% up to ₹8L, 10% up to ₹12L), standard deduction increased to ₹75,000, and the Section 87A rebate raised to ₹60,000 — making income up to ₹12 lakh completely tax-free in the new regime.

When does the Old Regime still win?

If your total deductions under the old regime exceed ₹3–4 lakh, run the comparison. At ₹15L salary with maximum 80C (₹1.5L), 80D (₹50K), home loan interest (₹2L), NPS (₹50K) = ₹4.5L deductions — old regime may save ₹15,000–₹30,000 more. Use this calculator to check your specific case.

Can I switch between regimes?

Salaried employees can switch regime every year by informing their employer before April. Self-employed and business owners with business income can switch only once after opting out of new regime. If you have no business income, you can switch freely each assessment year.

What is the 87A rebate in new regime for FY 2025-26?

The Section 87A rebate under the new regime is ₹60,000 for FY 2025-26. This means if your net taxable income (after standard deduction of ₹75,000) is ₹12 lakh or less, your entire tax liability is wiped out by this rebate. Effectively: gross salary up to ₹12.75 lakh = zero tax under new regime.
